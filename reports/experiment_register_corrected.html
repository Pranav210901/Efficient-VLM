alignment_vlm · experiment register · 2026

Every experiment, what it measured, and what it cost to learn.

A chronological register of the frozen-encoder vision–language alignment programme: twenty pre-registered experiments from the original BLF grid to the selected M‑T1 text-aggregation model, with results, best-in-experiment rows, losses, weights and method notes.

Main dissertation question: Can modern frozen SSL vision encoders, combined with compatibility-based pair selection and sub-5M-parameter adaptation, approach compact jointly pretrained vision-language models under a fixed inference budget? Hardware: RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell, native BF16 Compiled: 2026-08-04

Era I — Branch-Level Fusion on legacy encoders

to 23 Jul

The original dissertation question: do two lightweight fusion branches on a frozen vision tower improve image–text alignment? Answer: a little, unreliably.

Era III — The queue, and how it ate 19 points

28 Jul – 1 Aug

The single most consequential result in the project: a training-only memory queue, inherited unquestioned from v2, was suppressing every earlier number.

The pre-registered prediction said sigmoid loss would win. It didn't — queue-free InfoNCE did, by 0.80pp, and the loss family turned out to be worth ~1pp against the queue's ~19pp. The mechanism: queue entries are enqueued from a pre-update forward pass and never recomputed, so they are stale in a projection space that is itself the thing being trained. A frozen backbone doesn't protect you when the projector is the moving part. Every conclusion from the historical queued v2, v3 and v4 runs is therefore scoped to that queued recipe, not to frozen alignment in general.

Loss

Control
Matched-LR controls C/D added because sigmoid alone had received the original LR sweep (a 3–5pp tuning effect)
Audit
No detach/eviction/positive-mask defect found — a design mismatch, not a bug

Queue factorial — age vs capacity

Complete

QuestionIs the damage caused by how old queue entries are, or by how many there are?

Nominal age (steps)Image-only queueText-only queueMatched-drift finding

A queue holding embeddings one optimizer step old already cost 6.46pp — there is no safe non-zero age. The pre-registered "images decay faster" prediction was a clean miss, and the miss produced the better result: neither age nor measured projector drift alone predicts damage (drift R² 0.68–0.77 within a modality, 0.12 pooled). Drift is necessary, not sufficient. On efficiency the queue offered nothing either: no throughput gain, 8.9 GiB of 95 GiB used.

What holds the whole register together

  • Pre-registration with scored predictions. Wave 0 went 2/3, the queue factorial 2 hits / 1 miss / 1 no-prior — and both misses produced the better science. A post-hoc narrative would have absorbed them as confirmation.
  • Gates that were allowed to fail. v3 stopped at +1.42pp, the hard-negative guard excluded 99.64% of candidates, CC3M closed on a negative. None of these thresholds was lowered retroactively.
  • Sealed evaluation. Flickr30k test was touched once, for the frontier. Every other number in this register is development or validation evidence, and every selection-influenced figure says so.
  • Immutable branches. Each experiment writes to its own results/checkpoints/logs tree; v2 is locked by hash into every later manifest and has never been rewritten.
  • Weights are addressable. Every reported run carries a checkpoint SHA-256 fingerprint, GPU model, node, precision and software versions.
  • Two known soft spots. The learning rate has been pinned from projection-only training and untuned since; and checkpoint-epoch selection on Flickr validation is disclosed everywhere but never removed.
